# Field Note: The Product Became Easier to Explain

## Hook

Most operations tools show activity. PM Ops Map shows ownership.

## The problem

Property-management teams rarely lack another screen. They lack a shared answer to four questions:

1. What work exists?
2. Who owns it?
3. What is blocked or drifting?
4. How do we hand the picture to the next person?

## The build

Phase 2 gave the product one memorable promise:

**Map the work. Assign the owner. Review the gaps. Share the picture.**

The new navigation makes that promise visible in the interface. The documentation now says what the product is — and, just as importantly, what it is not.

## Why the positioning works

The old story asked the reader to understand a collection of features. The new story gives the reader a sequence. That sequence is easier to demo, easier to remember, and easier to extend without turning every new capability into a new product promise.

The Portfolio and Work Orders views still matter. They are supporting context, like the instruments around a cockpit: useful for decisions, but not the aircraft's identity.

## Interview-ready technical takeaway

This was not “just copy.” It was a product-surface refactor with a deliberately small blast radius. Stable view IDs and routing behavior stayed intact while labels, metadata, README structure, contributor boundaries, and roadmap language were aligned. The change improves discoverability without coupling the product story to a risky architectural rewrite.

## One-line share

PM Ops Map is the responsibility layer between “we have a lot of software” and “we know who owns the work.”
